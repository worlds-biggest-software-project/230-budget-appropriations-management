## Relevant Industry Standards or Protocols

- **GASB (Governmental Accounting Standards Board) Standards** — authoritative accounting standards for state and local governments in the US; GASB is developing digital taxonomy for GAAP financial statements
- **GFOA (Government Finance Officers Association) Best Practices** — widely adopted budgeting guidelines covering budget presentation, monitoring, capital planning, and performance measurement; GFOA Distinguished Budget Presentation Award widely sought
- **OMB Circular A-11** — federal guidance governing the preparation, submission, and execution of the US federal budget; applies to federal agency systems
- **FASAB (Federal Accounting Standards Advisory Board)** — accounting standards for US federal agencies; governs appropriations tracking and financial reporting at the federal level
- **COFOG (Classification of Functions of Government)** — UN international standard for classifying government expenditure by function; used in comparative and intergovernmental reporting
- **XBRL / DATA Act** — structured financial reporting requirements for federal agencies; digital taxonomy development ongoing at GASB
- **SEFA (Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards)** — required schedule for governments receiving federal grants; budget systems must support SEFA preparation

## Market Research

**Market Size:** Dedicated government budgeting and appropriations management software is a specialised subset of the broader government ERP and financial management market. The government software market broadly is multi-billion in scale; purpose-built government budgeting tools are a fragmented niche dominated by ERP suites (Tyler, Oracle, CGI) with specialised standalone tools (OpenGov, GovMax, Questica, Euna) targeting specific segments. No single consolidated market sizing figure is consistently cited across research sources.

**Funding:** OpenGov was acquired by Cox Enterprises in 2023. Tyler Technologies is publicly traded. Oracle and CGI are large enterprise software companies. Euna Solutions has private equity backing. The govtech ERP space sees consolidation rather than new entrant growth.

**Pricing Landscape:** Enterprise government ERP suites (CGI Advantage, Tyler Munis) carry multi-million dollar implementation and licensing costs for state-level deployments. Mid-market cloud platforms (OpenGov, Euna, Questica) range from $30,000–$300,000/year depending on agency size. GFOA compliance and budget book publishing features are increasingly standard expectations.

**Key Buyer Personas:** State and local government finance directors and budget officers; city managers and county administrators overseeing appropriations processes; legislative staff tracking budget appropriations against enacted authority; federal agency budget analysts managing OMB A-11 submissions; CFOs of government-adjacent entities (utilities, authorities, special districts).

**Notable Trends:** GASB is developing a digital financial reporting taxonomy, signalling a future shift to structured digital financial statements. Cloud-native budget formulation tools are replacing spreadsheet-based processes in mid-market governments. Performance-based budgeting and outcome linkage are increasingly required for GFOA budget award compliance. Real-time budget monitoring against actual expenditure is a top procurement requirement. Digital presentation of budget books for public transparency is now expected.

## AI-Native Opportunity

- AI-assisted budget narrative generation: LLMs could draft department budget justifications, executive summaries, and public-facing budget book narratives from structured financial data, reducing the staff time required to produce compliant GFOA budget documents.
- Scenario modelling and sensitivity analysis: AI models could generate rapid multi-scenario budget projections (revenue shortfall, inflation adjustments, new programme additions) and surface trade-off analysis for elected officials without manual spreadsheet rebuilding.
- Appropriations monitoring and variance alerting: AI agents monitoring actual expenditure against appropriated authority could generate real-time alerts when spending trajectories risk overrunning appropriations, enabling proactive corrective action.
- Natural-language budget query interface: Conversational AI could allow elected officials, journalists, and the public to ask plain-language questions about government spending and receive accurate, cited answers from authoritative budget data.
- Automated SEFA and grant compliance tracking: AI could map expenditures to federal award categories automatically, generating SEFA schedules and flagging potential matching fund shortfalls or allowable cost questions before audit season.
